Rebranding and UI/UX design: first impression does not repeat

Guide · Design · July 2026.

The buyer assesses your business in less than a second — by logo, colors, font, orderliness. This estimate determines how much they will trust you and how much they will be willing to pay. Design is not a decoration; design is the price you can charge.

UI and UX — What Is the Difference (in Human Language)

UI (Interface) is what things look like: colors, typography, buttons. UX (experience) is how the user feels when things are used: if he finds a price in three seconds or three minutes, if he knows where to click, if he is disturbed by form. A nice website with a bad UX is a nice shop where nothing can be found.

When is it time to rebrand?

  • The logo was made "by the way" ten years ago and it's embarrassing to put it on the table.
  • You look cheaper than the prices you want to charge
  • On each channel you look different (second color, second font, second tone)
  • You are aiming for a new, more paying market (e.g. diaspora or B2B)

What the Process Look like

  1. Detect Who you are, who you sell to, who you are different from, brand is the answer to these three questions.
  2. Identity: logo, palette of colors, typography + book of standards (that each future design looks "like you").
  3. UX folder and prototype: for the site/application we draw the user path and make a clickable prototype — before programmingThe change in the prototype costs an hour; the same change in the ready-made code costs days.
  4. Application: site, network, memorandum, email signature — all consistent.
