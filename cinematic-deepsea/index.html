Eight civilians. One year. A single unbroken descent aboard the EREBUS — to the floor of the ocean and back.

01 / SUNLIT SURFACE
0–200m

The epipelagic zone. The last place sunlight behaves like sunlight. Below the keel of the EREBUS, ninety-five percent of the ocean has never been seen by human eyes.

02 / SUNLIT ZONE
200m

A blue whale glides past the viewport — the largest heart ever to beat, the size of a small car. From here the water swallows red light first, then orange. The world turns monochrome blue.

03 / TWILIGHT ZONE
1,000m

Sunlight surrenders. In perpetual dusk, ghostly jellyfish pulse through the black. The EREBUS wakes its twin floodlights — the only stars this deep. Pressure: one hundred atmospheres.

04 / MIDNIGHT ZONE
3,000m

Total darkness. Then — sparks. Ninety percent of creatures here make their own light. The hull drifts through a living starfield of bioluminescence, each flash a language we cannot read.
